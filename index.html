How a project moves from approval to published results.

    The leadership team reviewed proposed secondary analyses and approved the projects listed here, checking for overlap between them.

  2. Preregistered

    We encourage every team to record its hypotheses and analysis plan in a public preregistration, for example on OSF, before analyzing the data. We add the link to the table once it's posted.

  3. Published

    Teams share their results publicly after the main paper is published in Nature, which protects the Registered Report review.

About the study

The Global Social Media Experiment tests the causal effects of social media use around the world. Participants in 23 countries were paid to cut back their social media screen-time for two weeks. We then measured how this changed their polarization, intergroup attitudes, well-being, news knowledge, and exposure to online hostility.

The main analyses are preregistered as a Registered Report that received in-principle acceptance at Nature, and the Stage 1 protocol describes the design in full. The dataset can answer many questions beyond the main paper, and the approved secondary analyses take on some of them.

Frequently asked questions

About these projects

Who approved these projects?

The study's leadership team reviewed proposals from collaborators and approved the projects listed on this page, checking for overlap between them.

Why don't some projects have a preregistration link yet?

Teams post their preregistrations as they finish their analysis plans. Until we've received a link, the table shows "Not yet available".

When will results from these projects be available?

After the main paper has been published. Teams hold secondary results, including preprints, talks, and posts, until then to protect the Registered Report review at Nature.

Can I get the data?

The anonymized dataset will be released publicly after the main paper is published. It includes participant-level survey data from all 23 countries, a codebook describing every variable, and the survey materials used in each country.

How should I cite the study?

Cite the main paper: Rathje, S., Asimovic, N., Ventura, T., et al. Testing the causal impact of social media usage around the globe. Nature (Stage 1 in-principle acceptance). We'll update this citation once the paper is published.

For project teams

My project is missing or its details are wrong. What should I do?

Email us with the project's name and the names of the people working on it, and we'll fix the table.

How do I add or update my preregistration link?

Email us the link at any time. We update the table regularly.

Do I need a preregistration?

It isn't required, but we strongly encourage it. Preregistering on a site like OSF before you analyze the data makes your secondary results more credible.

Can I use the data for analyses beyond my approved project?

Please use it only for your approved project. If you'd like to try something else, email the leadership team first so we can avoid overlap with other teams.

Can I share the data with students or colleagues?

Only with people working on your approved project. Don't post or redistribute the data before its public release.

When can I publish or present my results?

Please wait until the main paper has been published before sharing secondary results publicly, including preprints, talks, and posts. This protects the Registered Report review at Nature. You're free to analyze the data and write up your work in the meantime.

Another project looks similar to mine. Is that a problem?

The leadership team reviewed overlap when approving projects. If you think your work overlaps with another team's, let us know and we'll put you in touch.

Still have questions?

Email the leadership team at globalsocialmediastudy@gmail.com. We read every message, though it may take a few days to reply.
