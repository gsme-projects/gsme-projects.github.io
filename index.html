About the study

The Global Social Media Experiment tests the causal effects of social media use around the world. Participants in 23 countries were paid to cut back their social media screen-time for two weeks. We then measured how this changed their polarization, intergroup attitudes, well-being, news knowledge, and exposure to online hostility.

The main analyses are preregistered as a Registered Report that received in-principle acceptance at Nature, and the Stage 1 protocol describes the design in full. The dataset can answer many questions beyond the main paper. Collaborators proposed secondary analyses, and the projects selected by the leadership team are listed below.

How it works

Forecasts only count if they're made before anyone has seen the results, so the survey always comes before the data.

  1. Find your project

    Check that your project appears in the list of approved projects. If it's missing or the details are wrong, email us.

  2. Take the forecasting survey

    Predict the effects of reducing social media use on the study's main outcomes. There are no right or wrong answers, and your forecasts don't affect your access to the data.

    About 15 to 20 minutes

  3. Tell us about your project

    At the end of the survey, share with us the necessary information about a secondary analysis that you'd like to conduct (if you have one). If approved, we'll add it to the table on this page.

  4. Receive the data

    Once the forecasting window closes, we'll share the anonymized dataset.

Frequently asked questions

Projects

My project isn't listed. What should I do?

If your project was already approved, email us with its name and the names of the people working on it. If it's a new idea, describe it at the end of the forecasting survey.

How do I add or update my preregistration link?

Add it at the end of the forecasting survey, or email us the link at any time. We update the table regularly.

Do I need a preregistration?

It isn't required, but we strongly encourage it. Preregistering on a site like OSF before you open the data makes your secondary results more credible.

Can I propose a new project now?

Yes. Describe your secondary analysis at the end of the forecasting survey. If it's approved, we'll add it to the table on this page. Check the list first so your idea doesn't overlap with an approved project.

Two projects on the list look similar. Is that a problem?

The leadership team reviewed overlap when selecting projects. If you think your work overlaps with another team's, let us know and we'll put you in touch.

The forecasting survey

Why do I have to make forecasts before I get the data?

A forecast is only informative if it's made without knowing the answer. Collecting predictions from researchers across 23 countries before the results are known lets us measure how well experts anticipate the effects of social media, and which effects surprise them. Once you've seen the data, that's impossible to undo.

Does everyone on my project team need to take the survey?

Yes. Every collaborator who will work with the data should complete the survey individually, so we receive independent forecasts.

What will I be asked to predict?

How reducing social media use for two weeks affects the study's main outcomes, such as polarization, intergroup attitudes, and well-being. The Stage 1 protocol describes the outcomes if you'd like to review them first.

Do my forecasts have to be accurate?

No. There are no right or wrong answers, and your forecasts have no effect on your access to the data. We want your honest best guesses.

Can I discuss my forecasts with my team before submitting?

Please don't. Forecasts should reflect your own expectations, so answer on your own without comparing predictions with others.

Will my forecasts be identifiable?

Forecasts will be analyzed and reported in aggregate. We won't publish individual forecasts alongside names.

Can I change my forecasts after I submit?

No, submitted forecasts are final. If you made a mistake, such as a typo, contact us.

Data access and publishing

When will I receive the data?

After the forecasting window closes. We'll email everyone who completed the survey with download instructions.

What will the data include?

Anonymized participant-level survey data from all 23 countries, a codebook describing every variable, and the survey materials used in each country.

Can I use the data for analyses beyond my approved project?

Please use it only for your approved project. If you'd like to try something else, email the leadership team first so we can avoid overlap with other teams.

Can I share the data with students or colleagues?

Only with people working on your approved project. Don't post or redistribute the data. It will be released publicly after the main paper is published.

When can I publish or present my results?

Please wait until the main paper has been published before sharing secondary results publicly, including preprints, talks, and posts. This protects the Registered Report review at Nature. You're free to analyze the data and write up your work in the meantime.

How should I cite the project?

Cite the main paper: Rathje, S., Asimovic, N., Ventura, T., et al. Testing the causal impact of social media usage around the globe. Nature (Stage 1 in-principle acceptance). We'll update this citation once the paper is published.

Still have questions?

Email the leadership team at globalsocialmediastudy@gmail.com. We read every message, though it may take a few days to reply.
